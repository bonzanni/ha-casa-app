# Job triggers: a schedule that starts a plugin job

> Code is the source of truth. This file is a map; when it and the code disagree, the code wins.

## Scope

A resident's scheduled trigger (`interval`, `cron` or `date`) whose target is a plugin job
instead of a prompt: what the entry may carry, how it is checked when it is written, loaded and
registered, and what happens when it fires. How scheduled triggers in general are registered,
fired and owned is [`triggers.md`](triggers.md). What a job is, how it runs in batches in its
own topic and how its end is told is [`background-jobs.md`](background-jobs.md).

## Mental model

**A job trigger starts the job, not a turn.** An ordinary scheduled entry carries a prompt,
and its fire sends that prompt to the resident as a scheduled turn. A job entry carries
`job: <manifest name>:<job name>` instead, with an optional `task` and `context`. Its fire
sends nothing to the resident: it resolves the job's host at that moment, builds the origin a
job the operator started from their DM would carry, and starts the job through the same
launcher `start_job` uses. From there the job runs, posts and ends exactly like one the
resident started, and the resident hears of it only through the job's own end telling.

**The entry is checked where it is written, and the fire tells what it could not do.** The
schema makes `job`, `prompt` and `prompt_file` mutually exclusive on every scheduled type,
lets a `date` entry carry a job instead of a prompt, ties `task` and `context` to `job`,
forbids `clearance` on a job entry and requires its channel to be `telegram`; a webhook entry
may carry none of the three keys. The typed upsert refuses a job the resident cannot start now
(`job_not_declared`) and a channel other than Telegram (`job_needs_text_channel`), before
anything is written. Registration checks only the channel, never whether the job is
startable, so a job whose plugin was unassigned later can never stop Casa from booting.
Instead each fire that cannot start the job tells the operator once.

**The origin is the operator's.** A scheduled fire has no sender. The fire takes the live
operator identity and builds its clearance with the same constructor the DM ingress uses for
the authenticated operator, so the job reads, posts and recalls with exactly the access the
same job started from the operator's DM would have. With no operator configured there is
nobody to tell, and the fire only logs.

## Contracts & invariants

**INV-TRIG-022**: A scheduled trigger carrying `job` registers whenever its resident declares the channel, which for a job entry is `telegram` (schema and upsert refuse any other; never fatal to boot for an unstartable job); the typed upsert refuses a job the resident cannot start; at fire the job is launched through the one launcher `start_job` uses, with an origin carrying the live operator's identity and clearance, and no resident turn runs; a fire that cannot start the job tells the operator once — the admission decision as a past event at the occurrence — and runs nothing; the job then runs, posts and ends exactly as one the resident started, except that its launch prompt and briefs say a schedule started it.

That one difference is the starter line, `Started by: scheduled`, which the fire's
`_scheduled_job` stamp decides at launch ([`background-job-fresh-sessions.md`](background-job-fresh-sessions.md)); a job a
resident's own scheduled turn starts reads `agent` instead, because an agent turn decided it
(unless the operator answered a question in that turn).

What it does not cover: catching up an occurrence missed while Casa was down (no scheduled
trigger is caught up; see [`triggers.md`](triggers.md)); what the job does once started
([`background-jobs.md`](background-jobs.md)).

## Failure behavior

A fire that cannot start the job sends one labelled line to the operator and records the
same line in the resident's echo. The label is the hosting specialist's, or the resident's
when no host was found. Each line is a past event at that occurrence, bounded to the echo
line limit with the job's title clipped first:

- `… "<title>" did not start — no host was available at this occurrence.`
- `… "<title>" did not start — another job of this plugin held the claim at this occurrence.`
- `… "<title>" did not start — Casa was at its work limit at this occurrence.`
- any other refusal names its kind in parentheses.

Nothing is retried before the next occurrence. A failed send of the line is logged and not
retried. A one-shot `date` job entry is dropped from the scheduler after it fires, like any
other one-shot; the entry stays in the file (INV-TRIG-009 in [`triggers.md`](triggers.md)).

## Extension points

**Another channel for job triggers** needs the job's own channel requirement lifted first:
a job posts into a Telegram topic, which is why the entry is Telegram-only.
